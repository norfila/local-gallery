# User guide

How the app works, screen by screen. The sideloaded version is called **Gallery** and the Google Play version **Local Gallery**; they behave the same except where noted (see [Two versions](development.md#two-versions)).

## Views and controls

- **Library:** all accessible photos and videos, with search, sorting and metadata filters. Tap **Library** in the bottom navigation to return from another page to **All items**. Tap it again while Library is selected to open a floating shortcut strip above the navigation, with **All items**, **Photos**, **Videos** and **Folders**. Choosing a view closes the strip and updates the view label above the content. Tap Library again, tap outside the strip or press Back to dismiss it. The side menu also keeps dedicated Photos, Videos and Folders pages. Video thumbnails have a play indicator; videos play with Media3 (ExoPlayer) in the viewer.
- **Folders:** actual indexed device folders with covers and item counts, as a **grid** or a **list** (table rows with cover, name and item count). Tap the arrangement button beside the folder count for the **Folder arrangement**: Grid or List, **1–6 folders per row** (grid) and **rounded folder corners**, with a live preview. Masonry is not offered for folders. The choices persist across restarts. Tiles show only the folder name; open a folder and tap **i** next to its name for the storage location (copyable), contents and total size.
- **Albums:** virtual collections of media and linked folders. Folder links include subfolders and new indexed items. Files are never moved or copied; removing an album removes its links. Unlink a folder to exclude its items. **Album options** opens an Album settings sheet with actions styled like the main Settings menu.
- **Side menu:** tap the menu icon inside the search bar, or swipe inward from the left edge, to open Library, Photos, Videos, Folders, Albums, Map, Countries, Hidden (when turned on in Settings), Bin (Android 11+) or Settings. Lines separate the library views (Library to Folders), the collections and places (Albums to Bin) and Settings.
- **Countries:** browse photos grouped by their saved GPS country. Country lookup runs entirely on the device with bundled Natural Earth **1:10m** land boundaries (simplified by `scripts/build_countries.py`), which include small islands such as Praslin and La Digue in the Seychelles. It needs no internet and sends no coordinates to a geocoder. Points just outside the simplified coastline (beaches, harbours, boats near the shore) use the nearest country within **25 km**; open-sea points further away stay in "Unmatched GPS". Photos without GPS and photos whose metadata has not been scanned have separate groups. Country tiles are slightly taller than wide; the photo count sits in a small badge on the picture (a flag for countries, a crossed-out pin for the other groups) and the name, in the phone language, sits below. Dataset attribution is in `app/src/main/assets/countries-LICENSE.txt`.
- **Floating navigation:** Library, Albums and Map (German: Karte) float over the content in a solid, rounded capsule. Library's compact shortcut strip uses a separate pill with four small, labeled icons and pill-shaped selections. The strip is 56 dp tall at the default font size, with an 8 dp gap above the toolbar; it grows when larger text needs more room. Library has no caret. The selected bubble's edges stretch and settle while moving between destinations. The navigation capsule has no shadow or navigation-press ripple; the space around it is transparent. While Library is the open tab, a small chevron after its label shows that another tap opens these views; it points down while the strip is open. Photos, folders and albums scroll on underneath Android's navigation strip at the bottom edge instead of stopping above it.
- **Settings:** a full-screen page with softly rounded groups for **General**, **Appearance**, **Library & access** and **Map**. **General / Language** chooses English, Deutsch or the phone's language (**System default**); the app switches at once and stays in Settings. On Android 13 and newer the same choice also appears in Android's own per-app language setting. **Appearance** shows Light, Dark and System as three small pictures of the library, each in its own colours; tap one to use it. **Pure black** makes the background black in dark mode, for OLED screens; cards and menus stay slightly raised. Below them, **Colors** offers **System colors** (from the wallpaper, Android 12 and newer), Gallery's own **Sage** (the default) and Teal, Blue, Purple, Pink, Red, Orange, Olive and Gray as round swatches; the app, the pictures and the photo viewer's accents change at once. Both choices persist across restarts; light or dark also controls Simple maps and widgets, which keep their own colours. **General / Language** and **Start page** are rows that open their list wherever you tap them. **Map / Online map** switches the online map on or off (see [Simple and Satellite maps](#simple-and-satellite-maps)). Choose a **Start page** from Library, Photos, Videos, Folders, Albums, Map or Countries. It is used on the next normal launch; a widget or external photo request still opens its requested content. Photo and folder arrangement are not in Settings: they open from the button beside the item count on their pages, where the change can be seen. **Scan library** rescans without leaving Settings; **Media access** shows the current permission. Photo locations (Android's "media location" permission, needed to read GPS from photos on Android 10+) are requested together with photo access, so there is no separate GPS row; Media access notes it only if locations are still missing. **About Gallery** contains version, open-source, ratings, gesture and map information; **Website** and **Source code** open the Norfila page and the public repository in the browser; **Privacy policy** shows the bundled policy. Use the back arrow or system Back to return to your previous view.

Tap **Photo arrangement** beside the item count to choose **Grid**, **Masonry** or **List**, **1–6 columns** (grid and masonry) and rounded corners, with a live preview, a segmented layout choice and one-tap column buttons. **List** shows one item per row like a table: thumbnail, file name, date, dimensions or duration, size, rating and a GPS marker; selection (including drag selection) works the same. Pinch inward on the thumbnail grid to fit more columns; spread two fingers apart to show fewer, larger thumbnails. Column changes persist and apply across the library, albums, folders, country groups and media pickers. Grid uses square tiles; Masonry sizes tiles from item proportions, with limits for very tall or wide media. Two-finger layout gestures work alongside ordinary scrolling and swipe selection.

The Library header uses two compact rows: a rounded search field with the navigation and metadata-filter buttons inside (with active filters the filter button becomes a pill with the count, inset evenly from the field's edge), followed by the current view, item count, sort and photo-layout controls. Tap the field to open the **Search workspace**, or tap its filter button to open **All filters** directly. There is no separate view heading or permanent filter-shortcut row. The abbreviated sort control opens a themed menu with the full choices and a checkmark: **Newest first**, **Oldest first**, **Highest rated** or **File name**.

Photo and video thumbnails have **square corners by default**. Enable **Rounded photo corners** in the photo arrangement (the button beside the item count) for rounded edges. The choice applies immediately and persists across restarts, covering Grid and Masonry in the library, album/folder contents, countries and photo pickers. It changes the corners, preserving the chosen layout and image proportions.

Grid and folder thumbnails use Android's cached MediaStore previews on Android 10 and newer, with limited concurrent thumbnail generation and versioned memory-cache keys. Unsupported previews and older Android versions fall back to size-constrained original decoding. Opening a photo reuses its cached grid preview while loading the larger image. Whole-library filtering, sorting, month grouping and selection-index construction run on a background worker. Metadata updates are batched so they do not rebuild the grid five times per second.

## Hidden page

Photos and videos inside dot folders (such as `.thumbnails` or WhatsApp `.Statuses`), folders containing `.nomedia`, and dot files have their own **Hidden** page. They never mix with the normal library: Library, Photos, Videos, Folders, Map, Countries, search and widgets show only visible media. The one exception is an album you added a hidden item to yourself; folder links never pull hidden items into an album, and album widgets leave them out even then.

Turn on **Settings / Library & access / Hidden page** to add **Hidden** to the side menu, between Countries and Bin. The option is off by default. The page lists hidden media as folders, with the same folder arrangement (grid or list, folders per row, corners) as Folders; open a folder for its photos, with search, sorting, selection and the viewer working as usual. Back on the folder list returns to the library.

The first visit without access explains the ways in (the Google Play version offers only chosen folders):
- **Allow all files access** (full version, Android 11 and newer) opens Android's setting. Gallery then scans every storage volume by itself; no folder needs to be picked. On Android 8–10 the normal storage permission already allows this.
- **Choose a folder** allows a parent folder such as DCIM or Pictures in Android's folder picker, and works for other storage providers too. Already indexed local files are not duplicated.

The folder button in the page header opens **Hidden media access** later: the all files access status and the chosen folders, which can be added, allowed again when unavailable, or forgotten (this removes Gallery's saved access, without deleting anything). **Include thumbnail caches** stays in Settings, below the Hidden page switch.

All files access is a broad permission. It is fine for a sideloaded app, but Google Play only allows it for specific app types; chosen folders are the narrower alternative. Supported thumbnail image files are shown; thumbnail databases such as `.thumbdata` are not photos. Gallery only reads these files; it never changes `.nomedia` or unhides files in storage. Android's own bin (`.trashed-*`) and in-progress (`.pending-*`) files are skipped, as are other apps' private `Android/data` and `Android/obb` folders, which the folder picker cannot expose either.

The library appears first; hidden media is searched for afterwards, so the scan never delays it (the page shows "Looking for hidden media…" meanwhile). The scan result is cached for 10 minutes; **Scan library** rescans immediately, and the page refreshes on returning to the app. Turning the page off removes it from the menu, stops the scan and keeps the folder choices for later; settings from 0.15 and earlier carry over, so "Show hidden media" users find their hidden items on the new page.

Document-provider thumbnails are used when available; other images use size-constrained decoding. Metadata and filters work with these files. Moving arbitrary document-provider files is not supported. Hidden files can't go to Android's bin; deleting them asks for confirmation and removes them permanently (requires all files access, or write access to the picked folder). Folder links and album entries use saved document references; changing the granted parent folder may change those references.

## Photo editing and file moves

Open a photo and tap **Edit photo**. Two tools sit below the photo: **Pen** (colors, width, clear ink) and **Crop**. Crop shows the whole photo with a frame: drag a corner or an edge to resize it, drag inside to move it, with a rule-of-thirds grid while you work. The frame stays inside the photo and never gets smaller than about a fingertip. Ratio chips lock its shape (**Free**, **Original**, **Square**, **4:3**, **3:4**, **16:9**, **9:16**; a locked shape starts as the largest centred crop) and **Reset crop** returns to the whole photo. Undo and clockwise rotation work in both tools; rotating turns the crop with the photo. The editing area keeps clear of the system back gesture at the screen edges, and closing the editor (Back or ✕) with unsaved changes asks **Discard your edits?** first. **Save copy** creates a new JPEG in `Pictures/Gallery`; the original stays unchanged. The edited copy opens after saving. Editing works on an oriented, sRGB image with a maximum long edge of **4,096 pixels**, and JPEG export flattens transparency and animation. Available capture EXIF is copied and orientation/dimensions are updated; vendor fields, embedded ratings and original XMP regions are not copied. On Android 8–9 the system save picker chooses the copy's location. Video editing is not included.

**Move to folder** moves the actual photo or video to an existing compatible folder on the same storage volume, or creates a folder under Pictures (photos) or Movies (videos). Photos can live in `DCIM` and `Pictures`; videos in `DCIM`, `Movies` and `Pictures`. This is separate from adding items to virtual albums. Android asks for per-item write approval when required. Moving retains the MediaStore URI, file bytes and direct album links; membership through linked folders changes with the destination. Conflicting filenames are handled by Android without overwriting the existing destination. Moves require Android 10 or newer; cross-volume moves and arbitrary document-provider files are not supported. Canceling the move leaves the file where it is.

Destinations are chosen on a full page instead of a list in a popup. **Move to folder** shows the folders that hold media (the same ones as the Folders page; an empty Pictures or DCIM/Camera appears once something is in it) as tiles with a cover, the number of items, the name and the path (names such as Camera repeat), with **New folder** first. One tap on a folder moves the files and closes the page; a new folder is named in a small dialog whose **Move** button does the same. A note then offers **Undo** for a few seconds, also above an opened photo: it puts each file back into the folder it came from. Adding to an album uses the same kind of page with the album covers and **Create new album** first; one tap adds the photos and closes the page, and a short note names the album. The cross or Back leaves either page without changes.

Android asks for approval each time an app moves, bins or deletes media it did not create. On Android 12 and newer this can be switched off: **Settings / Library & access / Move and delete without asking** shows the state and opens Android's **Media management apps** page for Gallery. Once allowed there, moves and deletes go through without Android's window; deleted items still go to the bin for 30 days. Older Android versions keep asking. The first time Android's window has interrupted a move or delete, Gallery asks once whether to switch it off (**Open settings** or **Not now**); after that the choice lives in Settings.

Animated GIFs play when opened (Android 9 and newer also play animated WebP). Thumbnails in the grids stay still.

A whole selection can be moved the same way with **Move** in the selection bar. Android 11 and newer ask once for all items that need approval; Android 10 asks per item. Each item stays on its own storage volume. Photos and videos selected together can go to any `DCIM` or `Pictures` folder, and a new folder for them is created under `Pictures`; `Movies` folders are left out, because Android keeps no photos there. Items that cannot be moved (hidden files, or ones Android refuses) stay where they are, and the confirmation says how many moved and how many did not.

While items are selected, a labelled bar takes the place of the floating navigation: **Share**, **To album**, **Move** (to a folder) and **Delete**. Inside an album, **From album** (remove from this album, the files stay) takes the place of **To album**; an opened photo keeps its own add-to-album action there. The Hidden page has no Move. The top row keeps the close button and the count; the navigation returns when the selection ends.

Touch and hold an item, then drag across others to select a range. Once selection is active, swipe sideways across items to select or deselect more without holding again; vertical gestures still scroll. Near the top or bottom edge, dragging scrolls through the grid. While selecting in a date-sorted view, a checkbox beside each month selects or clears that month's currently visible, filtered items. A partially selected month shows an intermediate checkbox state. Selecting can be felt: a pulse when the long press starts it, and a light tick for each item a drag adds or removes, for a tap while selecting and for a month's checkbox. This follows the phone's touch-feedback setting.

To choose an album thumbnail, open the album, open **Album options**, and choose a cover from its accessible photos. **Reset automatic cover** restores the first available member. The same cover appears on album tiles and widgets. If the chosen photo is deleted, inaccessible or unlinked, Gallery falls back to an available album member. The cover is a saved reference and never changes the photo file.

Metadata filters combine `<`, `<=`, `=`, `!=`, `>=`, `>` rules for stars, ISO, aperture, focal length, resolution, file size, width, height and date. All rules must match. Also filter camera, folder and GPS presence, or search names/folder paths. Unknown numeric metadata does not match numeric rules. Rating 0 means unrated. Date comparisons use whole days in the device timezone.

The **Search workspace** combines text search with **Favorites** and shortcuts to Rating, Camera and Date. **Selected filters** shows the number of conditions in a small pill beside the heading; on the right, the star saves the combination as a favorite and the crossed-out filter icon removes all conditions at once (the typed search stays). Selected conditions use compact pills with the same text size and spacing as the other choices. The bottom result action shows the live photo/video breakdown, such as **Show 24 photos · 3 videos**, without repeating media totals above the filters. Counts run on a background worker. Tap this action or the keyboard Search action to return to the current library, folder, album or map with the selection applied. Search text and selected criteria survive activity recreation. Album-list search stays inline and searches album names.

**All filters** uses the same live selection, with editable/removable selected conditions above expandable Rating, Date, Camera & exposure, Location, Image size and Files & folders categories. It is reachable from the search field's filter button and the workspace's All filters row. Opened directly from the filter button, Back returns straight to the previous view. Opened inside Search, Back returns to Search. The toolbar arrow and Android Back behave the same way and keep the current query. Criterion editors stage changes until **Add filter** or **Update**; Cancel leaves the live query intact. Add another condition to create numeric/date ranges. The rating editor offers **0 ★ to 5 ★** as one row of chips (0 means unrated). The clear-all icon beside Selected filters removes all metadata criteria while retaining text search.

Tap the star beside **Selected filters** to save the combination, or the same star inside an editor to save just that condition. Saving a staged condition does not apply it. Favorites remember metadata only, and adding one preserves typed search and other criteria, deduplicating identical conditions. A different camera, folder or GPS choice requires explicit replacement confirmation. The star on an existing favorite opens its name dialog with Save and Cancel actions. The Favorites menu offers apply, a pen for renaming, and a bin for deleting a favorite. Existing saved filters are retained after updating.

There are no separate quick filters: they repeated the favorites. New installs start with the favorites **4+ stars**, **Unrated** and **With GPS**, which can be renamed or deleted like any other. When updating from 0.14 or earlier, each quick filter that was still switched on is added once as a favorite (unless a favorite with the same condition exists), the old "On map" default is renamed "With GPS", and the 12+ MP shortcut is dropped (it remains available under Image size). Favorites never apply automatically on launch. Active criteria also remain removable beneath the compact library header.

## Turning the phone

Turning the phone never closes anything: the screen re-lays out instead of being rebuilt, so an open photo, the editor (with unsaved edits), Settings and dialogs stay where they are, and a playing video keeps playing. The same holds when Android switches between light and dark mode. If Android does rebuild the screen (for example after a language change, or when it ended the app in the background), the open photo is restored once the library has loaded.

**Settings / General / Rotate only when viewing photos** (on by default, phones only) keeps the library, albums, map and settings upright; an open photo and the editor turn with the phone, following Android's auto-rotate setting. Switch it off to turn the whole app. Tablets always turn freely.

**Short screens** (less than 480 dp tall: a phone turned sideways, or split screen) make room for the photos: while you scroll on, the search bar, the row with the count and sort buttons, the filter chips and the floating navigation slide away; any scroll back brings them back at once. They stay in place while photos are selected, on the map, and while TalkBack is on. Tall screens, including tablets in landscape, keep them in place.

## Photo viewer and details

The viewer follows Google Photos and Aves. **Tap** the photo to show or hide all controls, including the system bars; the choice stays as you swipe to the next photo. While a video plays, the controls hide after three seconds.

- **Top bar:** back, date/time and position ("3 of 120"), and a **⋮ menu** with Details, Add to album, Move to folder, Open with, **Set as wallpaper** and Use as (contact photo and other targets). Set as wallpaper opens Android's own crop-and-set screen; many current wallpaper apps no longer answer the generic "Use as" request, which is why it has its own entry. On a phone without that screen, Gallery asks for Home screen, Lock screen or both and sets the middle of the photo in the screen's shape.
- **Bottom bar:** Share, Edit (photos) and Delete. For videos, a seek bar with play/pause, times and mute sits above it.
- **Swipe up** on an unzoomed photo, tap the small arrow above the bottom bar, or choose Details: the photo slides up and the details list appears below it. It shows the date, location (with **Open in maps**), the rating stored in the file (read-only, only when there is one), and metadata categories, which all start collapsed; tap a category to expand it. Drag the list down at its top, or tap the photo, to close it. Horizontal swiping keeps working while details are open.
- **Swipe down** on an unzoomed photo to close the viewer. Double-tap or pinch to zoom; at a zoomed scale dragging pans.

Videos use Media3 (ExoPlayer): the next video starts automatically, its thumbnail covers the screen until the first frame, and playback pauses when the app goes to the background.

## Delete and bin

Delete from the viewer's bottom bar or from selection mode (selection mode also offers Share). On **Android 11+**, items go to **Android's bin** after Android's own confirmation; open **Bin** from the side menu to see how many days each item has left (30 days in total), select items to **Restore** or **Delete forever**, or **Empty bin**. The bin is shared with other gallery apps. On Android 8–10 there is no system bin: Gallery asks for confirmation (and storage write permission) and deletes permanently. Album links of deleted items simply stop showing.

The reader shows available human-readable EXIF and parsed XMP. Large values are bounded, binary maker notes/image blobs are omitted, and unsupported vendor fields depend on AndroidX ExifInterface. Library GPS and location-bearing XMP require photo-location metadata permission. When another app explicitly shares a photo, Gallery shows only the metadata that app supplies in its readable stream.

## Home-screen album widgets

Open an album and choose **Album options / Add home-screen widget**, select **1 × 1**, **2 × 1**, **1 × 2** or **2 × 2**, then confirm placement with the launcher. These four presets also appear in Android's widget picker; place one and choose an album from the cover grid. Tapping a widget without a usable album (not chosen yet, or deleted) opens that album choice directly. With the System appearance, widgets switch between light and dark together with the launcher, even while Gallery is closed. All presets can be resized horizontally and vertically. They show a rounded cover and compact album label; larger sizes also show the accessible item count. Actual cell sizes, resizing and reconfiguration depend on the launcher. Each widget independently stores its album choice and uses that album's chosen cover. Tapping opens that album. Multiple widgets can point to different albums.

Widgets refresh after library/album/appearance changes and every 30 minutes, subject to Android scheduling. A refresh right after Gallery scanned the library reuses that scan instead of reading storage again. Deleted albums and denied photo access have explicit states. Removing a widget clears only that instance's configuration.

## Simple and Satellite maps

On the Map page the map is a rounded card, set apart from the search field and aligned with its edges and with the navigation capsule. The map card offers Simple, Satellite, fit-photo-locations and **Maximize map** controls. Maximize opens the map full screen; tap **Restore map** or press Back to return. The camera and layer choice are retained. Nearby photos cluster; tap to browse them. The map choice persists. Loading waits for native rendering; failures offer retry and a Simple fallback.

Simple uses OpenFreeMap/OpenStreetMap vector mapping and follows the app's theme. Satellite uses a **2025 Sentinel-2 composite**, with native zoom through level 14 and enlargement beyond it. It is not live aerial imagery. **Both map layers require internet to load new areas. Downloadable offline map regions are not implemented.** Some recently viewed tiles may remain in the map engine's cache, which is not a guaranteed offline map. Country grouping remains available offline. No key/account setup is required. The app reads photo GPS, without requesting the phone's current location. Photos and coordinates are not uploaded, but map providers receive tile requests for the viewed areas, which can hint at where photos were taken. This is explained in **About Gallery** and behind the shield button on the map.

**The map asks first.** Until you agree, the Map page shows a blurred placeholder drawn on the device with **Load the online map?**, which explains what is sent and to whom; no map code runs and nothing is downloaded. **Not now** keeps the map off (**The online map is off**, with **Load map**) without asking again on every visit. **Load map** remembers the choice. To switch it off again, use the shield button on the map (**Turn off online map**) or **Settings / Map / Online map**; switching it off also deletes the map images the map engine saved on the device. After updating from 0.20 or earlier everyone is asked once, also those who had dismissed the earlier privacy note.

Download size would depend on the selected area, zoom levels and map layer. As a rough reference, [MapLibre's offline guide](https://github.com/maplibre/flutter-maplibre-gl/blob/main/website/docs/advanced/offline-regions.md) gives **20–100 MB for a city center at zoom 10–15**; this is not a measured Gallery download. Higher zoom levels increase tile counts quickly. [OpenFreeMap's public service](https://openfreemap.org/) permits offline downloads of at most **3,000 tiles or 50 MB per download**, with at most four parallel requests and an app-identifying User-Agent; larger downloads need provider agreement. Satellite download size and permission would need separate checking.

Satellite credit: **EOxCloudless by EOX IT Services GmbH**, **Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2025**, [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). The public service is rate limited/as-is and permits personal/noncommercial use; commercial distribution needs an appropriate EOX license. See [provider terms](https://cloudless.eox.at/documentation/license) and [service information](https://maps.eox.at/). Simple attribution: [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/) and [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).

## Opening photos from camera and other apps

Gallery registers for Android's standard image **Open with** (`ACTION_VIEW`) and camera review (`android.provider.action.REVIEW`) requests. When Android offers an app chooser, choose Gallery. A sender-provided temporary read grant can open the selected photo without granting Gallery access to the whole library.

Whether a camera's thumbnail button offers Gallery depends on the camera app. Some Google/Pixel Camera versions explicitly select Google Photos; Gallery cannot replace a target that the camera hard-codes. The [Aves maintainer describes the same limitation](https://github.com/deckerst/aves/discussions/1977). Supporting Android's standard review action does not guarantee integration with every Pixel Camera version. Secure/lock-screen camera review is not registered or supported. This integration still requires verification with the camera/version on a physical phone. MediaStore URI aliases resolve to one library identity for album links; temporary providers are not saved as permanent album references.

## Ratings, permissions and storage

Gallery reads embedded XMP `http://ns.adobe.com/xap/1.0/` Rating and Windows EXIF Rating (`0x4746`)/RatingPercent (`0x4749`), preferring XMP when both exist. These ratings are shown in the grid, list and details and drive the rating filters and **Highest rated** sort. Gallery does not set or change ratings itself: rate photos in an editor that writes them into the file, then rescan. Ratings assigned in-app by versions before 0.14 stay in the app database but are no longer shown or used. Originals are never rewritten. External `.xmp` sidecars are not currently read.

Grant photo and video access on first use. Android 14+ selected-media access limits visible items and widget counts. Photo locations are requested with photo access; if they were declined, Map, Countries and Settings / Media access offer to allow them. Runtime media-access controls are available from Gallery settings.

MediaStore supplies indexed local/removable-storage photos and videos; arbitrary document providers and cloud-only libraries are not browsed. Gallery observes changes, refreshes on return to the app, and caches metadata by URI, modification time, size and GPS permission. New indexed items appear automatically. If an external editor or the system index has not caught up, use **Settings / Scan library**. A rescan cannot discover files the system media index has not exposed. Uninstalling/clearing app data removes albums, widget configuration, saved filters and preferences, while original media remains. There is no account sync or export.
